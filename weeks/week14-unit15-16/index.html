Grammar Focus 1: Unreal Conditionals with if

Imagining a different reality

We use unreal conditionals to talk about imaginary or unlikely situations.

Structure: If + simple past, Subject + would / could / might + verb

If clause (imaginary situation) Result clause (possible consequence)
If I found $40,000, I would keep it.
If I found $40,000, I wouldn’t return it.
If I found $40,000, I could buy a new car.
If I found $40,000, I might go to the police.
가정법 과거: If절에 과거형을 쓰고, 결과절에 would/could/might + 동사원형을 씁니다. 실제로 일어나지 않은 상상의 상황을 표현합니다.

Practice: Unreal Conditionals

    B: Of course I (buy) her a new one.

  2. A: If you (have) three months to travel, where (you / go)?

If절에는 과거형, 결과절에는 would/could/might + 동사원형을 사용하세요.

Common Errors: Unreal Conditionals

❌ If I will find $40,000, I would keep it.

✅ If I found $40,000, I would keep it.

가정법 if절에는 will이 아닌 과거형을 씁니다.

❌ If I found money, I will return it.

✅ If I found money, I would return it.

가정법 결과절에는 will이 아닌 would/could/might를 씁니다.

❌ If I have more time, I would travel.

✅ If I had more time, I would travel.

가정법에서 if절의 동사는 반드시 과거형이어야 합니다 (have → had).

Grammar Focus 2: Past Modals — should/would have + p.p.

Giving opinions or suggestions about past actions

Use should have or would have + past participle to give opinions or suggestions about actions in the past.

What should I have done? What would you have done?
You should have told her about it. I would have called him.
You shouldn’t have lied to her. I wouldn’t have texted her.
I should have studied harder. I would have invited him out for a meal.

📝 Difference: should have — advice / regret (“the right thing to do”)   vs.   would have — what you personally would have done in that situation.

should have + p.p. = ~했어야 했다 (객관적 조언/후회). would have + p.p. = ~했을 것이다 (내가 그 상황이었다면 했을 행동). 부정형 shouldn't/wouldn't have + p.p.도 함께 사용합니다.

Past Modals: Sentence Analysis

She should have gone on a guided tour.

She should have gone on a guided tour.

I shouldn’t have spent all my money on clothes.

I shouldn’t have spent all my money on clothes.

I would have called him right away.

I would have called him right away.

I wouldn’t have texted her.

I wouldn’t have texted her.

should/shouldn't have + 과거분사 = (~했어야 했다 / 하지 말았어야 했다) — 객관적 조언·후회. would/wouldn't have + 과거분사 = (~했을 것이다 / 안 했을 것이다) — 가정 상황에서 본인의 행동.

Grammar Focus 1: Reported Speech — Requests

Reporting what someone asked you to do

Use say / tell / ask + (object) + to + verb to report a request. For requests in question form (“Can you ...?”), use ask.

Original request Reported request
“Arrive on time for the meeting.” She said to arrive on time for the meeting.
She told me to arrive on time for the meeting.
“Don’t leave your bike in the apartment hallway.” He said not to leave my bike in the apartment hallway.
He told me not to leave my bike in the apartment hallway.
“Can you pick up some food on the way home?” She asked me to pick up some food on the way home.
요청의 간접화법: said to / told (someone) to + 동사원형. 부정은 not to + 동사원형. 의문형 요청(Can you...?)은 asked (someone) to + 동사원형으로 보고합니다.

Common Errors: Reported Requests

❌ She told me to don’t be late.

✅ She told me not to be late.

간접화법의 부정은 not to + 동사원형입니다 (to don’t ✘).

❌ He said me to arrive early.

✅ He told me to arrive early.

사람을 지정할 때는 told (someone) to를 씁니다. said 다음에 사람이 오면 안 됩니다.

❌ She asked me that I bring some food.

✅ She asked me to bring some food.

요청의 간접화법은 asked + (someone) + to + 동사원형입니다.

❌ He told to bring some ice cream.

✅ He told them to bring some ice cream.

told 뒤에는 반드시 사람 목적어(them / me / her ...)가 필요합니다. 빠뜨리면 비문입니다.

❌ She asked me to brought the food.

✅ She asked me to bring the food.

to 뒤에는 항상 동사원형이 옵니다 (brought ✘ → bring ✓). 보고문이라 해서 시제 변형을 하지 않습니다.

Grammar Focus 2: Reported Speech — Statements

Reporting what someone said

Use said (that) or told (someone) (that) + clause. Shift the verb one tense back and adjust pronouns and time expressions accordingly.

Direct statements Reported statements
“I’m not feeling well.” She said (that) she wasn’t feeling well.
“I have houseguests for the weekend.” She said (that) she had houseguests for the weekend.
“I made a tennis date with Kim.” She said (that) she had made a tennis date with Kim.
“I have planned an exciting trip.” She said (that) she had planned an exciting trip.
“We can’t come tomorrow.” They told me (that) they couldn’t come tomorrow.
“We will be out of town.” They told me (that) they would be out of town.
“We may go out with friends.” They told me (that) they might go out with friends.
평서문 간접화법: said (that) 또는 told (someone) (that) 뒤에 시제를 한 단계 뒤로 이동(am/is→was, have→had, made→had made, can→could, will→would, may→might). 대명사(I→he/she)도 변경합니다.

Common Errors: Reported Statements

❌ He said that he is sick.

✅ He said that he was sick.

간접화법에서 시제는 한 단계 과거로 후퇴합니다 (is → was).

❌ She said me that she was tired.

✅ She told me that she was tired.

사람을 지정할 때는 told를 씁니다. said 뒤에 사람이 바로 오면 안 됩니다.

❌ He told that he will come tomorrow.

✅ He said that he would come the next day.

told 뒤에는 사람이 필요합니다. will → would, tomorrow → the next day.

Pronunciation: Reduction of “had” and “would”

In spoken English, had and would are reduced to ’d:

Written Spoken Which is it?
She said she had made the bed. She said she’d made the bed. ’d = had (past perfect)
She said she would make the bed. She said she’d make the bed. ’d = would (future in past)

How to tell the difference:

  • ’d + past participle (made, gone, eaten) = had
  • ’d + base form (make, go, eat) = would

Reading: A Good Excuse for a Day Off Work

On average, U.S. employees take 4.9 sick days per year. Usually this does not cause any particular problems. But when employees take sick leave without a good reason, it can quickly become an issue. In fact, in one survey, 18 percent of employers said that they had fired an employee for taking days off without a good reason. The key is to understand what reasons are acceptable and what reasons are not. Generally, most excuses for sick days fall into one of three categories.

The most common reasons for not going to work are health-related. It would probably be OK to tell your boss that you ate something bad last night and that you have a stomachache. Of course you might not want to share the details of a health issue with your boss — after all, you do have the right to privacy. If you don’t want to be too specific, you can just tell your boss that you have a small medical issue and need to take the day off.

Household accidents are the second category of reasons for not going in to work. You might call your boss to say you slipped in the shower and hurt your knee. This is a common accident and one that your boss will sympathize with. However, if you are going to be out of work for several days due to an injury, it’s important to make arrangements with your employer. See if you can work from home, or at least make sure there is someone to cover your work.

The third type of sick day use isn’t really about illness, but it’s about something else you can’t control: transportation problems. The car might not start, there may be a terrible traffic jam, or there could be delays on the subway. Some employers may be sympathetic to absences due to transportation problems, but others may not. It’s important to know your boss and to understand whether he or she will accept an excuse like this.

Regardless of the reason for the sick day, there are a few things you can do to make missing work more acceptable to your employer. Try to keep sick days to a minimum. When you do need to take a sick day, give your employer as much advance notice as possible. Finally, never take a sick day if there isn’t anything wrong with you — the only good excuses are the ones that are true.

Today’s Takeaways

Key Points

  • Unreal conditionals: If + past, would/could/might + verb (imaginary situations)
  • Past modals: should have / shouldn’t have + p.p. (past regrets & advice)
  • Reported requests: told/asked (someone) to / not to + verb
  • Reported statements: said/told + tense shift (is→was, have→had, will→would)
  • Collocations: make a mistake, do a favor, get into trouble
